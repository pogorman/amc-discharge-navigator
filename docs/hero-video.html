Using it

  • In the deck: its end card is the deck's title card, and the next slide, the overview, plays the rest of it (from the demo video) and holds on Govern. That's how the demo video opens too.
  • On its own: it's silent and the captions carry it, so it works in an email, a Teams post or a looping booth screen.
  • If you talk over it: "A patient who's medically ready but still in a bed. Ask the agent what's blocking him, let it draft the paperwork, and every app sees the same plan. And all of it runs in your tenant, under your policies."

It follows the narration rules: model output is always a draft for clinicians to review and sign, previews are labeled, and the models are "model deployments in Foundry." The screenshots are the live apps on synthetic data, and the Barrier Tracker card is drawn, not a screenshot. The Deploy & govern scene lists what the platform enforces in a customer's own tenant. In this demo tenant og-svc is a Managed Environment but has no DLP policy yet, so if anyone asks, the policies are theirs to set.

Changing it

Edit hero/animation.html (the timeline is in each element's --d delay, in milliseconds), preview it in a browser, then render it again with the steps in the runbook. The screenshots are hero/agent-chat.jpg and hero/dashboard.jpg; retake them if the apps change.

The full demo video

A 12 min 0 s narrated, captioned cut of the whole demo, stitched on 2026-11-01 from O'G's beat recordings, so the demo can run without a presenter. It opens on this video's title card ("Discharge Navigator"), then a card with the use case and the solution, then this video's overview, then a card that moves into the details ("Now let's see it work.", with the seven chapters lighting up) and a card with the cast. Each beat then gets a chapter card, a "What you'll see" card that says what's coming and which pieces are involved, its recording in a frame like this video's (the whole window, at its own zoom, so you can always see where you are), captions underneath, and an amber speed badge wherever a wait is sped up. Beat 1 also carries a "Microsoft 365 Copilot · with Work IQ" tag at the window's top-right corner, so it's clear that's Copilot on its own, and beat 2 a "Microsoft Copilot Studio · agent in Microsoft 365 Copilot" tag for the agent.

At the moments that need explaining, the recording pauses (an amber "Paused" badge shows it): the rest of the screen dims, a teal box moves to each part in turn, and a note says what it is. Beat 1 pauses before the first prompt (Work IQ, the Auto model picker, Chat with Cowork and Code, the left nav, the prompt) and on the answer (the hold-up and the two emails it cites). Beat 2 pauses on the Agents list. Beat 3 pauses on the agent's setup in Copilot Studio, on the Spanish instructions (why Spanish) and on the prior-auth packet (the quote the payer asked for, and what's still missing); its scrolling has the stops between scroll-wheel turns cut out. Beat 6 pauses on the App (preview) tile. The close pauses on the architecture, a part at a time. Every recording's frame says "Synthetic data · recorded in a demo tenant · narrated by an AI voice (MAI-Voice-2.1, preview)".

It's narrated by an AI voice (O'G, 2026-11-03, ADR-0018): Grant, a voice on Microsoft's MAI-Voice-2.1 model (public preview) in Azure Speech, generated from the project's own Foundry resource with Entra ID sign-in, and the video's last line says so. The narration goes with the captions rather than reading them out, and follows the same guardrails. Where a line needs more time than the picture gave it, the build makes room: a pause, card or held frame stays up longer, or a sped-up stretch plays a little slower. The captions file is the narration, word for word.

The deck follows this video slide for slide (O'G, 2026-11-03): the same cards, the deck's own request and architecture slides, and a slide for each recording that plays its stretch of this video. Each slide's notes say what to say over it and where it sits in the video, and end with the narration the video speaks there. The deck plays the video muted, so you can still present it live. The build script keeps the two in step (runbook).
