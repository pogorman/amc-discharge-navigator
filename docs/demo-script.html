Demo script

The run of show: about 20 minutes: an opening on the deck, then 7 beats · updated 2026-11-03: the deck now follows the demo video, so the opening jumps to its slides by name (the title card, the use case, the overview, the cast and the architecture) before beat 1; Barrier Tracker is used live in beat 4, beat 5 asks the agent inside the Command Center, and Barrier Tracker's build plays as your recorded clip in beat 6 (ADR-0016) · apps and screens are explained in the user guide

The story in one line: Miguel Reyes is medically ready but still in a bed. After three slides that introduce the cast and how it fits together, the case manager finds out why, the agent drafts the two documents that unblock him, the nurse manager closes a barrier on her huddle board, and leadership sees it all in one view. Then the reveal: she built that board herself, by describing it. One plan, three kinds of builder, one platform.

  • Notifications off (Focus / Do not disturb in Windows, Teams and Outlook).

Narration guardrails

Don't say, anywhere in the recording:

  • "Foundry agent." It's a model deployment in Foundry.
  • Anything about RVUs.
  • Don't ask a model what model it is. Point at the Model: line instead.

Do say: every model output is a draft a clinician reviews and signs. And the preview items: Mistral Large 3 in Foundry, Apps in Copilot Studio (beat 6), and the Evaluate tab on a harness agent (only if you show it).

  • Draft, review, sign. Every model output is a draft a person reviews and signs. Keep it administrative, not clinical, and the medical-device question never comes up.
  • No RVU claims. Never say "Copilot raises RVUs." A CMIO will take that apart.
  • Never ask a model to name itself on camera. Mistral answered "GPT" in testing (KI-09). Show the model field instead (ADR-0005).
  • Say "hands the task to a model we chose in Foundry," not "a Foundry agent." It's a model deployment.

The beats

Each beat lists the screen, what to do, a suggested line to say, what you should see, and what to do if it goes wrong. Times are rough. Start on the deck: the opening introduces every piece in plain words before beat 1 shows any of them.

Opening: what you're about to see Set the scene

About 4 min · Tab 1, the deck: the title card, the use case, the overview, the cast, then the architecture (type its name after # in the address bar, or follow the links)

  1. The title card. Who you are and what this session is.
  2. The use case: academic medical centers, discharge planning, and the solution. Your own words are in its notes. Then "Here's the whole idea in 30 seconds."
  3. The overview, the hero video without its end card. It plays by itself when the slide opens (about 25 seconds, silent): the problem, the agent, the drafts, the apps, then the architecture in their tenant under their guardrails. It holds on its last frame; the line is in the slide's notes.
  4. The cast. One line per row, top to bottom. Each row gives the role first and the product second, so the audience knows what every piece is before they see it work. For the huddle board, just say the nurse manager uses it in a few minutes and you'll show how she made it at the end.
  5. How it fits together, near the end of the deck, where the video closes. About a minute, left to right: the people in Microsoft 365, the agent and the one copy of the plan in Power Platform, and the model deployments behind API Management in Azure. Point at the zones; don't read the labels. The slide's notes have a line for each zone.

"That's how it fits together. Now let's watch it work on one patient: Miguel Reyes, the patient you met in the video."

Expect
